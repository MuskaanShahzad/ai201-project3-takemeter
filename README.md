## What This Does

This project trains a classifier on posts from r/ClaudeAI, a public subreddit
about using Claude and Claude Code. The goal is to sort posts by how
substantial they are — separating quick reactions and bare questions from
posts that show real reasoning, specifics, or structure.

Candidate distinctions noticed while reading:
- **Length/structure** — a single sentence vs. a multi-paragraph write-up with
  headers or bullets.
- **Concrete specifics vs. vague ask** — cites exact numbers, commands, or
  links vs. gives no detail about what was tried or wanted.
- **Self-contained vs. media-dump** — fully explains itself in text vs. is
  basically just an image/video with a minimal caption.
- **Shows own reasoning vs. bare question** — explains what was tried and why
  vs. a context-free question.

---

## Label Taxonomy

### `build_log`

**Definition:** Describes something the poster built, ran, or tested themselves, with at least one concrete specific (a number, a command, a timeframe, or a quoted outcome).

**Example 1:**
> 9,000 commits in two months with Claude Code: 5 things we learned building an agent that's allowed to touch real WordPress and Joomla sites

**Example 2:**
> I made a Claude Code mod that shows your 5-hour and weekly limits above the prompt and warns you before you hit them. [...] The percent turns red when your current pace would use up the limit before it resets. [...] Context window fill, plus toasts at 75/90/100%. [...] Install (in terminal claude): /plugin install limits --marketplace Ryan-LT/claude-mods

### `discussion`

**Definition:** Expresses an opinion, explanation, or concern about a feature, practice, or event. Not primarily seeking help, and not reporting the poster's own tested build.

**Example 1:**
> Nested sessions. Just landed in a new app update and it's pretty neat [...] I'm really liking it so far.

**Example 2:**
> When did Claude gain the ability to directly edit, add, and delete files from Project Knowledge/context? [...] Today, without asking for my consent, it added, removed, and edited dozens of files for me at the end of the session. While nothing seems broken, this scares me a little.

### `bare_ask`

**Definition:** A question or request where the poster gives no concrete specific about what they tried or want — nothing to evaluate beyond the ask itself.

**Example 1:**
> what can i do with fable? i have 2 days left in my weekly limit

**Example 2:**
> how do we start new threads/chats under the same project to save tokens, or is this not needed? im confused as a new pro user

### The hardest boundary

**Which two labels:** `discussion` vs. `bare_ask`

**The decision rule I used every time:**

If the post is fundamentally seeking an answer or help — even when it includes personal reasoning or context — it's `bare_ask`, unless it also reports a concrete specific tied to the poster's own completed work (a number, a finished build, a tested outcome), in which case it leans `build_log`. A post that asserts a position and isn't actually asking for anything is `discussion`, even if it's phrased with a question mark.

Borderline example: "Is there something like folding at home but for extra quota/tokens? I know I could use it on open source [...] But is there some way I can use these tokens for something actually good [...] rather than having it loop over and over on my vibe coded apps trying to find random bugs or write useless tests." It has real reasoning behind it, but at bottom it's asking "is there a way to do X" with no concrete specific offered — so it's `bare_ask`, not `discussion`.

I stress-tested these definitions with 8 synthetic boundary posts generated by Claude. One gap showed up: a rhetorically-phrased question that states an opinion ("Why does everyone assume the context window matters more than retrieval quality? It clearly doesn't.") would have wrongly landed in `bare_ask` just because of the question mark. The rule above fixes it — it isn't actually asking for anything, so it's `discussion`.

## The Training Run

**Base model:** `distilbert-base-uncased`

**Settings:** 3 epochs, learning rate 2e-5, batch size 16, seed 42, max length 128 tokens.

**Device:** CPU (torch 2.14.1+cpu), 101 seconds. Recorded in `results.json` — a different machine running the same seed could get slightly different numbers since hardware does the arithmetic in a different order.

**Anything I changed from the defaults, and why:** Nothing — used the notebook's defaults as-is.

**Split sizes:** train 139 · val 31 · test 31 (70/15/15, stratified). Per-label counts in the test split: `build_log` 12, `bare_ask` 14, `discussion` 5. `discussion` is below the ~8 the notebook flags as thin — with only 34 `discussion` examples total, its test slice is small enough that its score will likely swing a lot between seeds.

## How I Used AI

**Moment 1**

- *What I asked for:* Before labelling anything, I gave Claude my three label

  post only counts as `bare_ask` if it's genuinely seeking an answer — a
  rhetorical question that asserts a position stays `discussion`, no matter how
  it's punctuated.

**Moment 2**

- *What I asked for:* After I labelled the first 20 posts cold on my own,
  I had Claude pre-label the rest of the 200 as I pasted raw scraped posts from
  r/ClaudeAI in batches, against the same three definitions.
- *What came back:* A label for every post, plus flags on the ones that
  genuinely sat on a boundary — including two I'd tagged with both labels
  myself (`bare_ask / discussion`), which Claude resolved using our own
  decision rule rather than guessing.
- *What I changed:* I read and corrected every single label before accepting
  it, rather than batch-accepting.

**Pre-labelling disclosure:** The first 20 rows of `labels.csv` are labelled
`cold` — my own judgement, no AI involved. The remaining 181 rows were
pre-labelled by Claude against the definitions above, then read and corrected
by me one at a time before being accepted; those rows are marked
`pre-labelled, checked` in the `note` column.

**Moment 3 (unit 6, Milestone 3 — agreement adjudication)**

- *What I asked for:* I disagreed with staff on 3 posts. For each one, I gave Claude the post, the staff's rules, my label, and the staff's label, and asked it to argue both sides without telling me who was right. This is the exact method Milestone 3 suggests for working alone instead of in a breakout group.
- *What came back:* Two arguments for each post, each one backed by a specific staff rule instead of just an opinion. For one post, it pointed out that I was going by the post's tone, when the staff's own rule says tone isn't what decides the label.
- *What I changed:* I made the final call myself on all three, and I agreed with staff each time. Looking at all three together, I noticed my own pattern: I kept picking the more personal-sounding label whenever a post felt emotional, even when the actual rule pointed somewhere else.

**Moment 4 (unit 6, Milestone 4 — diagnosing the misses)**

- *What I asked for:* I had my three-seed results, my confusion matrix, my Milestone 2 consistency numbers, and my Milestone 3 agreement results, but hadn't connected them. I asked Claude to help me work out why `build_log` and `discussion` were missing their targets, instead of just writing "the model did badly on these labels."
- *What came back:* Claude linked three things I had but hadn't put together: my consistency check (I kept mislabeling posts as `bare_ask`), my confusion matrix (the model also defaults to `bare_ask`), and my own prediction from Unit 5 that `discussion` vs. `bare_ask` would be my hardest boundary. Together, they pointed to two different problems: `build_log` had a labeling-consistency problem, and `discussion` simply had too few examples.
- *What I changed:* Instead of one general answer, I wrote down two separate causes with separate evidence. That's what led me to pick my Milestone 5 fix.

**Moment 5 (unit 6, Milestone 5 — finding what to relabel)**

- *What I asked for:* Before changing any labels, I had Claude scan all 92 of my `bare_ask` posts for anything I might have missed — numbers, timeframes, or signs of an opinion that would make a post `build_log` or `discussion` instead.
- *What came back:* A shorter list of 8 posts worth a second look, not a labeling decision.
- *What I changed:* I reread all 8 myself and made the call on each one. Only 2 needed to change; the other 6 I confirmed were labeled correctly. Claude found what to look at; I decided the label.

**What I predicted before I looked:**
I think my trained model will beat the baseline the most on `discussion` — the baseline never got a single one right (F1 0.000), probably because `build_log` posts are easy to spot just from definition keywords like "built" or "tested", but telling an opinion apart from a question is a much subtler call that needs actual training examples.

**A note on my definitions file:** `label_definitions.txt` still had the starter's placeholder labels (`analysis`/`hot_take`/`reaction`) in it when I first ran the baseline, so none of my real labels matched and it silently fell back to bare label names. My prediction was wrong. **Diagnoses**

**Criterion 1 — Overall accuracy.** This isn't a separate problem from Criterion 2 — it's downstream of it. `bare_ask` alone is ~45% of the test set and already scores 0.65-0.72 F1, so accuracy is being dragged down specifically by `build_log` and `discussion` underperforming, diagnosed below.

**Criterion 2 — Per-label F1.** Two different labels, two different causes.

*`discussion` (F1 = 0.000 on all three seeds) — too few examples.* `discussion` is my smallest label: 34 of 200 posts, about 24 in training and only 5 in the test split. I flagged this exact risk in `criteria.md` before training even started ("I expect it to be my smallest label with the jumpiest test-split score"). The confusion matrix confirms it's not confused — it's just never predicted: on every seed, 100% of real `discussion` posts get called `bare_ask` (seed 42: 5/5, seed 7: 5/5) or split between `bare_ask` and `build_log` (seed 2024: 3/5 and 2/5) — never correctly. This is also the exact boundary (`discussion` vs. `bare_ask`) I called out as my hardest one in the Label Taxonomy section before I ever trained anything. The model found the same boundary hard that I did.

*`build_log` (F1 swings from 0.286 to 0.588 across seeds) — a labeling-consistency problem, and I have direct evidence for it.* My own Milestone 2 consistency check found all 4 of my mismatches went the same direction: posts I originally labeled `bare_ask`, I relabeled `build_log` (3 of 4) or `discussion` (1 of 4) on a blind second pass. That means my training data likely has some real `build_log`/`discussion` posts sitting in `labels.csv` mislabeled as `bare_ask`. That lines up exactly with what the confusion matrix shows: across all three seeds, `build_log` gets called `bare_ask` far more than the reverse (7/12, 10/12, 8/12 — and `bare_ask` is never once predicted as `build_log`, on any seed). If some of my `build_log` training examples were themselves originally mislabeled `bare_ask`, the model would learn precisely this lopsided bias. My staff agreement check (Milestone 3) points the same direction from a second angle: all three of my disagreements with staff were cases where I defaulted to the more generic-sounding label (`reaction`) instead of checking the specific rule that would've pointed to `analysis` or `hot_take`. That's a different taxonomy, so it isn't direct proof about my own 200 labels, but it's evidence of a real habit in how I label — reaching for the label that requires less to be true — and the direction of the model's bias toward `bare_ask` (the label that by definition requires the *least* — "no concrete specific") matches that habit exactly.

**Criterion 4 — Consistency.** The cause is the same boundary problem as `build_log` above, just measured directly instead of inferred: I don't consistently check whether `bare_ask`'s actual requirement ("no concrete specific offered") is true before reaching for it — on a blind second pass, 4 of my 20 posts moved from `bare_ask` to a label that did have a concrete specific (`build_log` 3 times, `discussion` once). That 80% isn't just a number against a target; it's the direct evidence behind the `build_log` diagnosis above, which is why I could call that one more than a guess.
